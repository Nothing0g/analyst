FILE / 03Readout

Keep the MVP close to the insight.

The goal was a focused response to a user problem, not a broad fitness platform with unrelated features.

01 / RESEARCH

Start with a behavior question.

The work considered what might explain disengagement for college-age users before choosing a solution direction.

02 / PRODUCT

Choose one intervention.

Competition became the central design lever, expressed through social comparison and small progress milestones.

03 / PROTOTYPE

Make the idea concrete.

A Thunkable MVP helped communicate the experience. The available project summary does not report a measured retention or engagement lift.

FILE / 04Reflection & next test

Test the behavior, not just the screen.

A prototype makes the hypothesis discussable. It does not show that the hypothesis changes real-world engagement.

  1. Make the sample visible. Document recruitment, question wording, and how survey responses were coded.
  2. Test with users. Observe whether social competition feels motivating, irrelevant, or discouraging to different students.
  3. Measure responsibly. A follow-up pilot could compare repeat use and self-reported motivation, with consent and clear privacy boundaries.
