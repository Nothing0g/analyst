FILE / 03Readout

Three signals worth carrying forward.

The case is intentionally narrow: turn a time-based EDA into a better operations question.

01 / TIMING

Hour-of-day is the real demand lever.

Demand ranges from about 1,320 bookings at 4 AM to about 12,400 at 6 PM. The shape repeats across weekday and weekend periods.

02 / FAILURE

Raw failure counts would mislead.

Busy hours naturally create more failed bookings. Rate-by-hour shows the underlying failure rate remains around 24–26% instead of tracking the demand curve.

03 / CAVEAT

The dataset needs an honest label.

Flat day-of-week behavior, even pickup geography, and tight fare/distance bands suggest a synthetic or simulated dataset. The finding is useful as an analytical exercise, not an organic Uber benchmark.

FILE / 04Ops recommendation

Fix the matching system, not just the rush hour.

The data does support a change in the next question an operations team should ask.

  1. Measure matching failure by segment. Break the flat aggregate into service type, pickup zone, driver availability, and cancellation reason before changing peak staffing.
  2. Use hourly demand for capacity planning. Keep the 6 PM peak visible, but treat it as a volume forecast rather than proof that it causes the failure rate.
  3. Validate against organic operational data. Re-test the finding on real dispatch logs before translating it into a production intervention.
FILE / 05Method & source

Dataset

150,000 NCR ride bookings across 365 days, with booking status, timestamps, pickup/drop locations, fare, distance, and cancellation reasons.

Approach

Parsed timestamps into hour, day-of-week, and month fields; compared demand by time, booking status, geography, fare, and distance; and used rate rather than raw count for failure diagnostics.

Tools

Python · pandas · NumPy · Matplotlib. The interactive view is a static portfolio rendering of verified repository findings, not a live production feed.

Limitation

The repository explicitly flags the dataset as likely synthetic or simulated because weekday/weekend counts, pickup concentration, and fare/distance bands are unusually flat. That caveat is part of the analysis, not hidden from the reader.
