01 / KINEMATICS

45° keeps the math legible.

F_horizontal = F_downward × tan(45°), giving a theoretical 1:1 force transfer before surface friction is included.

02 / MATERIAL

Low friction, high scrutiny.

POM/Delrin supports smooth unlubricated sliding at μ≈0.20, while the latch-hook fillet remains the primary stress-riser focus.

03 / CAVEAT

Prototype still matters.

The FEA is a static validation result. Tactile feel, friction, fatigue, and real spring/door interaction require the planned SLA prototype.

FILE / 04Design decision

Choose the reliable path, then test the feel.

The selected wedge system won because it reduced assembly complexity without accepting the long-term creep risk of the compliant flexure.

  1. Prototype the 3-piece system. Use SLA printing to check button travel, tactile click, latch release, and spring-assisted door ejection.
  2. Refine the wedge angle. Compare the reported 45° starting point against real friction and wear behavior.
  3. Keep the fillet change. Increase the inner latch-hook radius by 0.5 mm to distribute bending stress and improve durability.
FILE / 05Method & source

Brief

Design a battery door mechanism for two AA batteries. The input is downward normal force; the output is an automatic pop-open door after the latch clears.

Concepts

Compared a 5-part multi-pivot lever, a compliant flexure, and the selected 3-part wedge-driven system using degrees of freedom, assembly, tolerance risk, fatigue, and user feel.

Materials & BOM

PC/ABS housing and door; POM/Delrin wedge sled and latch; stainless micro coil springs. Estimated high-volume assembly cost: approximately $0.60 per unit.
